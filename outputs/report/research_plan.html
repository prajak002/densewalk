What the existing V-JEPA work actually predicts

From ~/latent2rgb (Horizon Ladder), read from its adapter and README rather than assumed:

PropertyFactConsequence for this project
ModelV-JEPA2 ViT-L/16, 256 px, frozen, action-free (not V-JEPA2-AC)It can't be asked "what happens if the robot does X". Navigation has to use it as a forecaster of the scene, not as a planner.
Encoder outputTokens [T/2 × 16 × 16, 1024]: one 1024-d token per 16×16 patch per 2-frame tubeletA grid of patch features, not a human state. No positions, no identities.
Predictor's native taskMasked spatiotemporal completion: predict tokens at hidden positions from visible ones within an observed clipFilling in hidden regions is in-distribution. That matches your "unseen things" requirement.
How latent2rgb queries itCausally: context = past tubelets, target = the one tubelet containing frame t+k. One forward pass per horizon, not an autoregressive loop.This query is out-of-distribution, and the repo says so itself. "Multi-step prediction" means several independent single-shot queries.
Measured quality of future predictionsThe predicted latent sits at 0.79 on a 0 (exact) to 1 (unrelated clip) scale, and moves only 0.016 between k = 2 and k = 32. Error after re-injection shrinks with horizon.As queried, the predictor barely uses its context. The prior is that these latents don't carry usable per-person future motion. Test this before building anything downstream.
DecoderA deliberately weak linear pixel decoder. It loses to copy-last-frame at short horizons (0.1807 vs 0.1663 RMSE).Pixel decoding can't be the interface to the scene. Use a task probe instead.
So "next-frame prediction" is the wrong description. The existing system predicts the latent patch tokens of one future 2-frame tubelet per query, from a frozen predictor queried outside its training distribution, with no action input and no human-level output.
Status 3

What in the proposal holds up, and what doesn't

Sound

  • Keeping Level 1 (world-model accuracy) and Level 2 (robotics use) as separate claims and metrics.
  • Static scene plus controllable walking humans. Excluding vehicles and other dynamics.
  • 3DGS by default, with 4DGS only if an experiment shows it's needed.
  • Treating occlusion failures as a failure mode to measure, not the whole thesis.
  • ~20 reconstructed scenes chosen by explicit criteria; the large dataset stays for Level 1.
  • A simple TTC planner before any VLA or new policy.

Needs changing

  • The closed loop in §6/§8 mixes two different loops. If the predicted human state is written into the scene and then re-observed, the model is watching its own predictions. That's an imagination rollout, useful for measuring compounding error. The robotics loop is different: the world advances by ground truth, and the robot acts on predictions and re-observes. Both are needed, measured separately (see formulation).
  • "Apply V-JEPA output to the scene" has no interface yet. The output is 256×1024 patch tokens. The smallest missing piece is a trained probe from tokens to per-person ground-plane position. That probe is the real Milestone 7.
  • The existing evidence already points to a limitation. Saturated latents that ignore context are the "genuine limitation" the brief asks us to show before changing the model. Put the test in week 2 as a go/no-go gate, not in week 8.
  • Render gap is missing. In the loop, V-JEPA sees splat renders with pasted humans, not real video. Its accuracy on renders has to be measured against the same moments on real frames, or the Level 2 results won't carry over to reality.
  • The planner baseline is too weak right now. In the existing 120-episode test the rule-based controller collides in 50% of episodes and PPO in 44%. With a baseline that weak, any gain from prediction is lost in the noise. Fix the baseline first.
Your two new requirements map well onto V-JEPA. Unseen regions: predicting hidden patches is V-JEPA's native training task. Occluded-region completion is therefore the strongest use of the model here, stronger than future prediction. Single-frame end to end: the encoder accepts one image (the library repeats it to fill a 2-frame tubelet). One frame carries no velocity, though. The loop should take one new frame per step into a rolling context buffer of 8–16 frames. That keeps the loop frame-by-frame without asking a single still image to tell you how fast people are walking.

Answers to the 10 decision questions

#QuestionAnswer
1What does V-JEPA predict?1024-d latent patch tokens at masked or target positions of a 2-frame tubelet grid. Natively for hidden positions inside an observed clip; causally (out of distribution) for one future tubelet per query. Action-free.
2Can it represent human future motion?Unknown, with a negative prior: future latents are saturated near chance. Gate G1 in week 2 decides.
3Can it map to 3D human state?Only through a trained probe: predicted tokens plus each person's current box → that person's ground-plane position at t+h. Supervision comes from tracks lifted with the ground plane. Train one probe on current tokens and one on predicted tokens, so the predictor's own contribution is isolated.
4Is 4DGS needed?No for the core loop. 4DGS bakes in the original people's motion, which conflicts with controllable humans. 3DGS + explicit trajectories is enough. 4DGS is only an optional render-realism ablation.
5Does SOMA add control?Only when humans must follow trajectories that were not recorded (counterfactual paths or poses). Replaying recorded tracks works with the real-pixel cutouts we already have. Check SOMA's license and how it renders against gsplat before committing; any SMPL-family body with retargeted walking cycles does the same job.
6Minimum scene complexity?One street, 10–30 walking people at moderate density, a walkable corridor ≥ 3 m wide, the robot within ~1 m sideways of the original camera path (where the splat is trustworthy). Varanasi already meets this.
7Minimum humanoid setup?What exists: the G1 flat-ground velocity policy (1500 iterations), a ground-plane collider, and people as analytic obstacles. No new locomotion training.
8Simplest closed-loop experiment?Replayed humans, robot on a receding horizon. Each step: render the robot's view → V-JEPA → probe → predicted positions → TTC planner → G1 step → humans advance along their recordings. Compare predictors with the planner held fixed.
9Which failures matter?Not known yet. Log occlusion fraction, crowd density, crossing angle and render quality for every frame, then rank failure types by frequency × impact. Occlusion is the hypothesis, not a result.
10What's necessary vs interesting?See the component table in Component roles. Cut: VLA, new PPO variants, optical flow, new depth models, 4DGS in the core loop. Deferred: SMPL/SOMA humans, reactive humans.
§25.1–2

Problem statement and hypothesis

In crowded pedestrian streets a humanoid has to act on people's near-future positions and on regions it can't currently see. We test whether a frozen video JEPA (V-JEPA2) supplies either one well enough, through a minimal learned readout, to improve navigation inside a controlled reconstruction of a real street.

Hypotheses (each tested on its own)

  1. H1, hidden regions (native task). Filling in occluded ground-level patches with V-JEPA2 lowers the false-free-space rate in occluded cells, compared with treating them as unknown-free or using a nearest-visible fill.
  2. H2, human futures (out-of-distribution task). For 0.33–2.7 s ahead, a probe reading predicted tokens gets lower per-person FDE than both constant velocity and the same probe reading current tokens. If this fails with the frozen predictor, a small causal predictor trained on frozen encoder tokens passes.
  3. H3, downstream use. With the planner held fixed, replacing constant-velocity futures with probe futures raises success rate or lowers collision rate in closed loop. The gain shrinks as horizon, density and occlusion grow.

Each is falsifiable, and each is reported with scene-level bootstrap CIs. A negative H2 is still a publishable finding about frozen video JEPAs, given the evidence already collected in latent2rgb.

§25.8 · §8

Closed-loop formulation: two loops, kept apart

Imagination rollout for Level 1–2 error growth

Ĥt+1 = g(f(o≤t), Ht)
ôt+1 = R(S, Ĥt+1, poset+1)
Ĥt+2 = g(f(o≤t, ôt+1), Ĥt+1) …

Predictions are rendered and fed back. Measure: FDE against ground truth over the steps, the step where FDE first exceeds 0.5 m, and whether error compounds or saturates (latent2rgb's Stage F saw it shrink). This is the loop described in §6. It tests self-consistency, not navigation.

Receding-horizon control for Level 2–3 usefulness

ot = R(S, Ht, poset) ← real state
Ĥt+h = g(f(o≤t), Ht)
at = argmina J(a; Ĥ, goal)
poset+1 = G1(poset, at), Ht+1 = replay

The world is always ground truth; only the robot's beliefs come from the model. Re-observing every step lets it recover from a wrong prediction. Measure: success, collisions, clearance, time to goal, stops, and the recovery rate after a large prediction error. This is why closed loop matters more for robotics: open-loop FDE punishes errors the robot would have corrected a step later.

Cost: J = wg·(remaining goal distance) + wr·Σi,h exp(−TTCi/τ) + wc·max(0, dsafe − dmin) + wo·(predicted-obstacle cells on the path). Humans are replayed, so they don't react to the robot. That's a stated limitation; reactive crowds are out of scope.

§25.9 · §11–12

Dataset strategy

Level 1: large data, no reconstruction

  • AV2 pedestrians: ego-moving video with 3D pedestrian cuboids from lidar. Metric ground truth for Head B, and lidar ground truth for Head A (the occluded ground is revealed later). Re-download sequences from the public S3 bucket.
  • WildTrack (already on the Mac, 4.8 GB): dense crowd with ground-truth world positions. A static camera, so it's a pure crowd-dynamics test.
  • dense_walk_mini (215 clips): egocentric crowd walking. Check the frame rate first: keyframes are 6 Hz, and V-JEPA2 was trained on denser video.

Level 2–3: 20 reconstructed scenes

Selected by thresholds written down before reconstructing:

  • SfM registers ≥ 90% of frames
  • Novel-view PSNR ≥ 22 dB with a gapped hold-out, not every-8th-frame interpolation
  • ≥ 5 people with tracks ≥ 3 s
  • Stature–depth correlation < 0.4 after the ground fit
  • Walkable corridor ≥ 3 m
  • Speed check passes: median walking speed 0.9–1.5 m/s, or a correction is recorded
  • Spread over density, occlusion and scene type

Order: 5 moderate → 10 denser → 5 stress scenes (crossing flows, heavy occlusion).

§25.10–11 · §16

Experimental protocol and baselines

  1. Freeze the splits by scene/clip before training any probe. The 20 reconstruction scenes never appear in probe training.
  2. Level 1: open-loop Head A and Head B on held-out clips, real frames only.
  3. Render gap: the same probes on renders of the same moments (camera on the original path), then with robot-viewpoint offsets of 0, 0.5 and 1 m sideways and head height 1.2 m.
  4. Imagination rollout: 20 scenes × all start times.
  5. Closed loop: 20 scenes × 3 goals × 10 start offsets = 600 episodes per condition. The planner and its weights are fixed across conditions and tuned once, on B2 only.

§25.12 · §17

Metrics, by level

A · world model

  • Per-person ADE/FDE (m) at each horizon, and miss rate at 0.5 m
  • Skill vs constant velocity: 1 − FDE/FDECV, split by visible vs occluded people
  • Head A: false-free-space rate and IoU in occluded ground cells, plus calibration (ECE)
  • Latent cosine to target tokens, secondary only, always reported with the chance baseline

B · scene / simulation

  • Render gap: probe FDE on renders minus on real frames
  • Human placement error: reprojected foot point vs detection (px and m)
  • Splat PSNR/LPIPS with a gapped hold-out, and PSNR as a function of distance from the camera path
  • Imagination rollout: steps until FDE > 0.5 m, and the growth slope

C · robotics

  • Success rate, collision rate, minimum human–robot distance
  • Time to goal, path-length ratio, freezing rate (stopped > 3 s)
  • Unnecessary stops (stopped while oracle TTC > 3 s)
  • Recovery rate after a prediction error > 1 m
  • All with scene-level bootstrap 95% CIs; no claim without a CI

§25.14 · §9

Failure analysis

Every closed-loop episode logs, per step: occlusion fraction in the planning corridor, density within 5 m, crossing angle of the nearest person, render confidence (distance from the camera path), prediction error per person, and the planner's chosen action. Afterwards:

  1. Label each collision or near miss with one cause: missed person, wrong future, hidden obstacle, planner error, render artifact, or locomotion.
  2. Report the frequency of each type, then its impact (drop in success rate when that type occurs).
  3. Fit failure probability against occlusion and density (logistic model, scene random effect).
  4. Occluded-hazard test ("the pond"): insert hidden static obstacles behind crowds in 20% of episodes and measure the false-free-space rate with and without Head A.
  5. Recovery: of the episodes with a prediction error > 1 m, how many still reach the goal because the planner replans?

§25.18–19

What could be novel, and what isn't

Could be novel (literature check first)

  • A controlled comparison of a frozen video JEPA's in-distribution use (masked completion of hidden regions) against its out-of-distribution use (causal future prediction) for the same downstream robot task
  • Occlusion safety measured as false-free-space rate against ground truth revealed later, rather than image similarity
  • Closed-loop humanoid evaluation inside reconstructions of real dense streets with controllable replayed humans, with the render gap measured instead of assumed away

Check against V-JEPA 2 / V-JEPA 2-AC, Navigation World Models, Vid2Sim, SplatSim, human-Gaussian work (HUGS), CrowdNav / SocNavBench, and ego-centric forecasting benchmarks (JRDB). None of these has been read for this plan yet.

Not novel

  • Using 3DGS or 4DGS as a simulation backdrop
  • Pasting humans into splats
  • A TTC planner, or the G1 walking
  • Constant-velocity pedestrian baselines
  • Demo videos without statistics
  • Adding a VLA or 4DGS "because it's more advanced"
