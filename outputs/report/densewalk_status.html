Why the 29.17 dB is misleading. Every 8th frame of one continuous walk is held out, so the nearest training frame is 0.27 s away. That measures interpolation, not novel views.
The clip's timebase is off. The spatial numbers agree with each other: near-field people measure 1.62–1.74 m tall. Speeds don't. The median pedestrian walks 1.74 m/s, where a crowded market is about 1.2 m/s, so the video looks sped up. All crowd speeds are divided by 1.45.

Side track · this week

Forecasting horizon on the Cosmos3-Nano DCA model

real-data run lost

Goal: predict where the walker will be Δt seconds ahead, conditioned on Δt, using the fine-tuned model's 4096-d last-token hidden state. The model predicts the walker's own action and nearest-obstacle distance, not per-object tracks.

The DCA gate improves action metrics a little but gives up most of the LoRA variants' obstacle sensing. All variants still let through more than half of unsafe "go" decisions.

A model that sees only the current state beats constant velocity by just 8% at 3 s, so there isn't much room for the latent to add value. Keyframes are 6 Hz, so Δt = 0.1 s can't be labelled on real data.
